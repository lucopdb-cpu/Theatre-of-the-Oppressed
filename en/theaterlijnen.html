Feeling, thinking, doing · Stanislavski · Brecht · Boal
Stanislavski asked the actor to feel what the character feels. Brecht asked the spectator to think about what they see, and to understand that it could be otherwise. Boal had the spectator stand up and do it themselves: "Theater is action!" Feeling, thinking, doing: this is the movement in which the Theatre of the Oppressed stands.

It is not a ladder on which each rung replaces the one below. "That dichotomy, Stanislavski versus Brecht, never existed," Boal wrote, and: "Emotion is part of knowledge." At the Teatro de Arena in São Paulo he set up a laboratory in 1956 to study Stanislavski methodically. He directed his first Brecht around 1960 with metalworkers, and that is where he began to think about the non-actor. In the forum it all comes together: whoever steps in feels the situation, thinks about what could be different, and tries it out. (our reading)

Formaat began in 1999 with the same Brecht learning play as Boal, played as forum. In this way this Arsenal, too, stands in that movement.

"Mostly everything comes from over there," Boal said in 2007, "Stanislavski, Brecht, Grotowski, Artaud." He called the Theatre of the Oppressed the first method from the southern hemisphere to be practised in the northern one. This page follows how he stayed in conversation with the makers from over there. Some lines are well documented, others are brief or critical, and for a few makers there is no line, only kinship.
