TO in the world — background
How Theatre of the Oppressed takes shape in different regions. The atlas with 12 practices worldwide, plus a regional carousel per continent. This is background material — context, history, networks, debates. You will find the toolkit itself on the main page.

Practices in context

12 places · Americas · Europe · Africa · Asia · conflict · training

Twelve practices, chosen so that no two answer the same question. One shows where the method was systematised; others show how it changed in exile, in the countryside, after civil war, among people without a home, in the face of ecological threat. Each card gives the context, four steps in how the work grew, and a Joker's tip. Not a directory, but a cross-section.

TO in MENA

13 cards · Middle East · North Africa · in Dutch

A double arrival in a world that already had its own theatres of resistance. Five strands — the Levant (Ashtar Ramallah, the Gaza/Syrian Monologues, Combatants for Peace, the Freedom Theatre in Jenin), the Maghreb (halqa architecture, Al Halqa Forum), Tunisia/Egypt (theatre of the revolution), Iraq (Al-Azraki) and Iran (Aryan Rezai's تئاتر شورایی) — closing with Khalid Amine as a critical counter-voice.

TO in Asia

14 cards · five strands + Plastow · in Dutch

Into which room did Boal arrive — and who was already sitting there? Five strands, older roots. Indian (Jana Sanskriti, Shadow Liberation, Sambhaavnaa/CCDC), Himalayan (Aarohan Nepal, Ajoka Pakistan, IRC), Filipino (PETA/BITAW), Southeast Asian (Drama Box Singapore, the Mekong network, Indonesia), and East Asian — with a card of its own for Korea (madanggeuk, han/shinmyŏng) and one for the Sinophone world with Hong Kong (APTFS, TEFO, Mok Chiu-yu) as the pivot, plus Taiwan and Shanghai. Closing with Plastow's warning in an Asian context.

TO in Central America

7 cards · REDTOCA · feminism as the main artery · in Dutch

A region that organises itself around REDTOCA — from FOMMA in Chiapas and METOCA in Quetzaltenango to La Cachada in San Salvador and the Magdalenas in Matagalpa. Indigenous languages as working languages, civil war as the context, the Magdalena method dominant.

TO in Brazil

11 cards · institutional home port · in Dutch

The institutional home port of TO — from Teatro de Arena and the sistema coringa (São Paulo 1956–1971) through CTO-Rio (1986–present) and Boal's years as vereador to the MST's Brigada Patativa, the CTO da Bahia (2025) and the Escola de Teatro Popular (Julian Boal & Geo Britto, 2017).

TO in Spanish-speaking South America

11 cards · the parallel continent · in Dutch

The parallel continent where Forum (Lima/ALFIN 1973) and Invisible Theatre (Buenos Aires 1971–76) were actually invented — from Buenaventura in Cali to Yuyachkani in Lima, with RELATO as the umbrella network, the Magdalena circuit and the Chile–Netherlands axis through Repetur.

TO in Oceania

13 cards · four traditions + explicitly Boalian practice · in Dutch

Into which room did Boal arrive? Four parallel traditions: academic applied theatre (Griffith/Melbourne), Indigenous-controlled theatre (Te Rākau, Whare Tapere, Ilbijerri, Marrugeku), Pacific community theatre (Wan Smolbag, Stages of Change, Raun Raun), and an explicitly Boalian practice in Melbourne and beyond (Reardon, Cañas, Morelos, ActNow Adelaide, plus Magdalena Australia and Aotearoa). Closing with Looser, Hereniko, Royal and Baldwin's empirical finding that Forum failed in Papua New Guinea.

TO in North America

14 cards · five streams + an Indigenous parallel · in Dutch

The region outside Brazil where Boal worked most personally — 22 years of tours, residencies and conferences (1987–2009). Five parallel streams: the academic infrastructure of PTO Inc. (Paterson, Cohen-Cruz, Schutzman, Kuftinec), New York praxis (TOPLAB + TONYC), Theatre for Living in Vancouver (Diamond's 2013 rebrand), a Latinx parallel (Teatro Campesino since 1965, before Boal!), and Playback Theatre (Fox & Salas, Hudson Valley 1975). Plus Marc Weinblatt's Playback–Forum hybrid at Mandala in Port Townsend, Mixed Company in Toronto as Canada's first CTO, and the francophone Quebec line with Ondinnok. Closing with Schutzman, Diamond and Lines et al. as the counterpart to Plastow.

On the horizon: all nine regional deep dives can now be opened; for now they are in Dutch. Some are still under active revision on the basis of more recent research files — Europe is scheduled for a third version, and Spanish-speaking South America and Brazil have a new part of sources that has not yet been fully worked in. The Caribbean, the Russian-speaking world, and Eastern Europe beyond Berlin remain blank spots — not necessarily gaps, but also signs of where the international TO literature itself is lopsided. TO in Oceania and TO in North America are both secondary mapping without substantial knowledge of the field — although for North America, Luc has had some indirect contact through Aristizábal's network and the PTO conference.
