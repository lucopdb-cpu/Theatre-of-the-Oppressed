A Joker's Arsenal

Theatre of the Oppressed Toolkit
Exercises, techniques, methods, attitudes and contexts from the Theatre of the Oppressed tradition. From Freire's pedagogical humus to Boal's classic games and present-day applications. For Jokers, facilitators and anyone who uses theatre for social change.
The English version is a work in progress. The TO Arsenal is available in English; the other sections open in Dutch for now.
Read the prefaceClose the preface

Anyone who draws the tree of the Theatre of the Oppressed starts with the roots: ethics, solidarity, history. From them grow image, sound and word. On the trunk stands the arsenal of games and exercises, and above it Image Theatre. In the crown are the forms many people know: Forum Theatre, Newspaper Theatre, Invisible Theatre, Legislative Theatre, the Rainbow of Desire. And right at the top is what it is all about: direct social action.

This Arsenal takes its name from that trunk, but it is never about the exercises themselves. An exercise only makes sense when it sets something in motion, in a group, in a neighbourhood, in policy. As a social worker, I see it as my task to make visible what remains invisible, to put injustice on the agenda, and to return what is experienced as a personal problem to where it belongs: in society. Putting issues on the agenda and politicising them are part of the work. Perhaps they are even its heart.

Multiplication

Knowledge that stays in one studio disappears with that studio. That is why the tree also holds multiplication: what you learn, you pass on, so that new trees can grow elsewhere. That is why this Arsenal is public, and why anyone may use, share and adapt it.

The roots

This content is not mine alone. It grew over twenty-five years at Werkplaats Formaat (a Rotterdam workshop for participatory drama), together with professionals, volunteers and above all the participants of countless workshops, ateliers and performances. And with all the people who contributed to Formaat's international masterclasses and connections. They are the roots of this work. The Joker above this page was drawn by Augusto Boal in the guest book at the opening of Formaat's studio. This Arsenal is a gesture of appreciation to everyone who shared this journey.

What it is, and what it is not

This is neither an introduction nor a recipe book, but a deepening for those who already work with groups. An exercise never works the same way everywhere. That depends on the source group, the moment and the conversation afterwards. The Arsenal is also incomplete: a selection, seen through a Dutch and European lens, and with gaps.

A beginning

Whoever reads this is not a spectator. Use what you find here, change it, and pass on what you discovered. The Arsenal will never be finished. No spect-actors, no social action.

Luc Opdebeeck, Rotterdam, first version, October 2026

What does the Joker do — attitudes that carry the work

6 cards · the act before the tool · in Dutch

Before the Joker picks up any tools: six attitudes that carry the work. First the question of what you actually do when you are the Joker — not which tools you know, but which act takes place in you. Then Community-Led Research and Action as a research position (the community does its own research, the Joker facilitates). Trauma-informed practice with SAMHSA's four Rs. Consent-based facilitation and brave space (Arao & Clemens, Rohd). Origin and the Joker as guest: what you do when you borrow from traditions you do not come from (Ramose, Smith, correspondence with Arun Naicker of Umtapo Centre). And finally, the position in relation to community arts (ICAF, Mind the Gap, Drama Box, PETA): a related register, a different promise. Not methods but attitudes — the basis on which all the other tools can work.

Methodological innovations

8 cards · Santos · J. Boal · Ganguly · Rubin · Alon · Thompson · Jackson · Diol · in Dutch

During Boal's lifetime, and certainly after his death in 2009, Jokers have developed the method further within specific cultural and political contexts: feminist-intersectional practice (Berlin), rural Forum systematisation (Calcutta), structural dramaturgy (Rio), policy-oriented Legislative Theatre (NYC and UK), polarised theatre between former enemies (Israel/Palestine), applied theatre and care (Manchester), artistic quality as recognition (London), and the forum in one's own circle with ecology as its starting point (Dakar).

Participatory drama as long-term group work

8 cards · Formaat Rotterdam · 1999–2025 · Opdebeeck · in Dutch

A Dutch contribution to TO: a way of working that Luc Opdebeeck shaped during his artistic leadership at Werkplaats Formaat (a Rotterdam workshop for participatory drama), in which the same group meets weekly for months. Boal's tools in an institutional setting, Freire's pedagogy as the ground, Moreno's logic of action as a forerunner. From Buurtatelier Delfshaven to Maia's in de Stad, with the Movisie method description and the research into quality of life as its foundation.
