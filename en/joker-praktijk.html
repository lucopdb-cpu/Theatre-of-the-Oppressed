Joker practice
foundation · CLRA · trauma-informed · consent-based · origin · community arts
Six attitudes about where the Joker stands, even before picking up any tools. First the question of what a Joker actually does — not which techniques they master, but which act takes place in them. Then: Community-Led Research and Action as a research position — the community does its own research and keeps control, the Joker facilitates. Trauma-informed practice as awareness: how do you prevent good intentions from re-traumatising people? Consent-based facilitation and brave space as a principle of form: the space is built, not assumed. Origin as the position of a guest: what does the Joker do when borrowing from traditions they do not come from themselves? Community arts as a related register: when do you work as a Joker within the logic of Forum, and when within the broader community-arts tradition? Not methods but attitudes. The basis on which all the other tools can work.

Joker practice — Six attitudes that together form the basis of contemporary Joker work. Not methods, not techniques, but epistemological and ethical positions that carry any Forum practice — or undermine it when they are missing.
